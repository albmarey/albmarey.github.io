42 Madrid Game Jam
Whilst I was at 42 Madrid, the programme’s first-ever Game Jam took place, and some of my fellow students persuaded me to take part. This project is the story of my first game jam.
It turns out that being ill, pizza and working for 48 hours straight make for a rather unusual combination.
The story
Basically, the theme of the Game Jam was colours. Our team of three, including myself, decided we’d make a roguelike in the style of *The Binding of Isaac*, with a single level where the enemies and doors would be colour-coded.
One of us created the 2D assets and the combat logic; we later realised that these could be created in black and white and the colours changed within the engine, rather than creating them individually. My second colleague was in charge of the entire interface and the player controls. Finally, I took charge of the procedural map generation, as that was what I knew best.
We worked separately, keeping track of our progress, and on the final day, about three hours before the deadline, we decided to merge our results. It turned out that we’d been working on different versions of Unity. When we merged everything, our projects crashed, and whilst trying to sort it out, we ended up losing some files.
In the end, we decided to hand in the project anyway, adding clown music and a world map with endless enemy spawns, so at least something was salvaged.
This was one of the most enjoyable experiences I had whilst learning the art of game development.
